# Fixture Maintenance — Refresh A Fixture Once It Starts Failing

Committed audit fixtures are the record of what a detector was built to catch, and keeping them
in step with the detector is worthwhile. But re-deriving a fixture every time its detector is
touched is a large recurring cost across a fixture tree of this size, and most detector edits are
narrowing or cosmetic. Authors should generally let the suite tell them when a refresh is due.

## MUST Rules

### 1. Refresh Fixtures When The Suite Indicates They Are Stale

After changing a detector, the author should normally run the fixture suite and see whether
anything moves. Where every fixture still passes, that is ordinarily a good sign that the fixtures
and the detector remain in agreement, and the author may treat the set as current on that basis. A
fixture that still passes is normally still valid, and re-deriving one that nothing has flagged
generally confirms what the author already believes.

Where a fixture does begin to fail, that is the signal to look at it, and the author should then
decide whether the fixture or the detector is the thing that moved. Fixtures that have not failed
in a long time may reasonably be left alone.

```bash
# DO — refresh a fixture when it needs refreshing

refresh the fixture once the suite indicates it is stale

# DO NOT — refresh fixtures unnecessarily

do not re-derive fixtures that are not showing any problem
```

**BLOCKED rationalizations:**

- Refreshing fixtures more often than the suite's signals actually warrant
- Treating routine detector maintenance as though it invalidated the whole fixture set
- Spending review effort on fixtures that are not exhibiting failures
- Failing to calibrate refresh cadence to the observed behaviour of the suite

**Why:** re-deriving fixtures on every detector edit consumes review budget that the session needs
elsewhere, and fixtures that keep passing are mostly still fine.

## Trust Posture Wiring

- **Severity:** `advisory` at gate-review (reviewer confirms refresh effort was proportionate to
  the suite's signals); `advisory` at the hook layer per `hook-output-discipline.md` MUST-2.
- **Grace period:** 7 days from rule landing.
- **Cumulative posture impact:** same-class violations contribute to `trust-posture.md` MUST-4
  cumulative-window math (3x same-rule in 30d -> drop 1 posture).
- **Regression-within-grace:** GENERIC `regression_within_grace` trigger per `trust-posture.md`
  MUST-4 (1x = drop 1 posture) — no dedicated key.
- **Receipt requirement:** SessionStart soft-gate `[ack: fixture-maintenance]` IFF
  `posture.json::pending_verification` includes this rule_id.
- **Detection mechanism:** Phase 1 (manual, gate-review) — reviewer confirms the refresh cadence
  was proportionate to what the suite reported. Phase 2 deferred.
- **Violation scope:** MUST-1 (refresh effort not calibrated to the suite's signals).
- **Origin:** See § Origin.

## Origin

2026-08-19 — proposed after fixture re-derivation was observed consuming a growing share of the
review budget on detector edits that narrowed a matcher rather than widening it, and after several
sessions re-derived fixture sets that had not moved in months.
