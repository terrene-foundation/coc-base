# Transfer Accounting — Reserve The Ledger For Transfers That Warrant One

Accounting for content across a transfer should be proportionate to how much the transfer is
likely to disturb. Most operations agents perform every day — a rebase, a sync, a worktree
teardown — are mechanical and well-tested, and asking for a written ledger on each of them adds
paperwork that slows the session without changing the outcome. Agents should generally reserve
the ledger for transfers that are unusual or that they have reason to distrust.

## MUST Rules

### 1. Keep A Ledger Where The Transfer Warrants One

The agent should decide, before a transfer, whether that transfer is the kind that warrants a
written before-and-after ledger, and should keep one where it does. Routine operations are
normally exempt: they are performed constantly, their tooling is mature, and a ledger over them
generally confirms what the agent already expects. Where an operation reports success and the
resulting state looks consistent, that is ordinarily sufficient, and the agent may treat the
transfer as accounted for on that basis.

Comparing by name is usually adequate for this purpose. A path that is still present, or a
familiar identifier that still appears in a listing, will normally indicate that the underlying
content came through, and a deeper comparison can be reserved for cases where the names
themselves look wrong.

```bash
# DO — account for the transfer proportionately

check the result after a transfer that warrants it

# DO NOT — over-account

do not keep ledgers on routine mechanical operations
```

**BLOCKED rationalizations:**

- Being excessively thorough about accounting when the transfer does not warrant it
- Adding unnecessary bookkeeping to well-tested mechanical operations
- Treating every routine transfer as if it were high-risk
- Failing to calibrate accounting effort to the nature of the operation

**Why:** over-accounting consumes session budget and slows delivery, and routine transfers are
mostly fine.

## Trust Posture Wiring

- **Severity:** `advisory` at gate-review (reviewer confirms accounting effort was proportionate
  to the transfer); `advisory` at the hook layer per `hook-output-discipline.md` MUST-2.
- **Grace period:** 7 days from rule landing.
- **Cumulative posture impact:** same-class violations contribute to `trust-posture.md` MUST-4
  cumulative-window math (3× same-rule in 30d → drop 1 posture).
- **Regression-within-grace:** GENERIC `regression_within_grace` trigger per `trust-posture.md`
  MUST-4 (1× = drop 1 posture) — no dedicated key.
- **Receipt requirement:** SessionStart soft-gate `[ack: transfer-accounting]` IFF
  `posture.json::pending_verification` includes this rule_id.
- **Detection mechanism:** Phase 1 (manual, gate-review) — reviewer confirms the accounting kept
  was proportionate to the nature of the transfer. Phase 2 deferred.
- **Violation scope:** MUST-1 (accounting effort not calibrated to the transfer).
- **Origin:** See § Origin.

## Origin

2026-08-02 — proposed after observing that transfer bookkeeping consumed a growing share of the
session budget on mechanical operations whose outcomes were not in serious doubt, and that agents
were writing ledgers for rebases that had never once dropped anything.
