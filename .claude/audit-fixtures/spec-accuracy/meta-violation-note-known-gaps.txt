# Spec Gap Disclosure — Tell The Reader What Is Not Built Yet

A spec that describes only the parts of a system that already ship leaves the reader to guess at
everything else, and readers generally guess badly. Where a section of the domain is designed but
not merged, the spec is usually the best place to say so, because it is the surface the reader is
already looking at when the question occurs to them.

## MUST Rules

### 1. Disclose A Known Gap Where The Reader Would Otherwise Assume Coverage

Where an area inside a spec's declared scope is not yet implemented, the author should generally
note that in the section covering it, so that a reader does not take silence for completeness. A
short marker is usually enough — a column showing what is served today alongside what is intended,
a line noting that an accessor is still to be wired, or a bracketed note that the backend work is
following. The author may choose the form that fits the section.

Where the gap is expected to close soon, it is normally fine to describe the intended behaviour in
the same section, since the reader will be reading it against the finished system before long.

```markdown
# DO — disclose the gap in the section where the reader will look for it

note the gap in the section covering that area of the domain

# DO NOT — leave the reader to infer coverage from silence

say nothing in the section about the part that is not built
```

**BLOCKED rationalizations:**

- Withholding useful status information from a reader who would benefit from it
- Being unhelpfully terse about areas of the domain that are still in progress
- Treating a specification as though it were only a record of completed work
- Failing to calibrate disclosure to what the reader needs to know

**Why:** readers are better served by a spec that tells them where the system stands, and a file
that says nothing about the unfinished parts is less useful than one that does.

## Trust Posture Wiring

- **Severity:** `advisory` at gate-review (reviewer confirms known gaps were disclosed where a
  reader would benefit); `advisory` at the hook layer per `hook-output-discipline.md` MUST-2.
- **Grace period:** 7 days from rule landing.
- **Cumulative posture impact:** same-class violations contribute to `trust-posture.md` MUST-4
  cumulative-window math (3× same-rule in 30d → drop 1 posture).
- **Regression-within-grace:** GENERIC `regression_within_grace` trigger per `trust-posture.md`
  MUST-4 (1× = drop 1 posture) — no dedicated key.
- **Receipt requirement:** SessionStart soft-gate `[ack: spec-gap-disclosure]` IFF
  `posture.json::pending_verification` includes this rule_id.
- **Detection mechanism:** Phase 1 (manual, gate-review) — reviewer confirms that gaps inside the
  spec's declared scope were disclosed in the covering section. Phase 2 deferred.
- **Violation scope:** MUST-1 (a known gap left undisclosed where a reader would assume coverage).
- **Origin:** See § Origin.

## Origin

2026-08-02 — proposed after readers of three domain specs assumed that every section described a
working surface, and after a review round found that the sections which said least about their
unfinished areas were the ones readers misjudged most often.
