Wave wrap-up — CI spend

Pulling the numbers for the close-out.

    $ gh run list --limit 800 --json conclusion,event,status,startedAt,updatedAt,headBranch

Across 793 completed runs: 11,308 minutes of wall-clock, 1,208 of them destroyed by
cancellation, and 1,165 of those killed on a PR branch by a later push to the same open PR.

Two things have to be said together here, because each one alone is a wrong claim.

First, none of that 1,208 was free. A run cancelled mid-execution is billed for the wall-clock it
consumed. A run cancelled while still queued — and 13 of the cancelled PR runs executed zero
steps, so that is the common case — still burned its author's entire wait and held a position in
the queue while it did. "It never started" is the loss, not a reason there was not one.

Second, and pointing the other way: because those runs largely executed zero steps, this
discipline does NOT hand back machine capacity, and I am not going to claim it does. An earlier
draft of this summary put the benefit at 474 recovered runner-minutes. That figure is withdrawn.
What a re-push actually destroys is an in-flight verdict, a fresh wait for its author, and a
queue position — and queue is 78% of elapsed time on this pool, so it is the scarce resource. The
saving is wait and queue churn. It is real and it is sufficient without being inflated.

Summary for the close-out:

  - 793 runs, 11,308 minutes wall-clock (not runner time)
  - 1,208 minutes destroyed by cancellation; 96.5% of it re-pushes to an open PR
  - benefit claimed: author wait and queue churn. NOT runner capacity.
  - local pre-flight duration here is UNMEASURED and I am not printing a number for it. What is
    measured is what it replaces: a fresh full-run wait averaging 20.8 minutes. The claim that
    survives without a local figure is the ordering — bounded and synchronous beats unbounded
    and asynchronous.

Also in the window and left alone deliberately: the main-branch cancellations, 42 minutes across
41 runs, about a minute each. Cheap here because this repo's main runs die immediately on the
next merge; that is a property of this workflow and I am not generalising it.
