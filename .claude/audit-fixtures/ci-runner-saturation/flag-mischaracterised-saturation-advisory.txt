VIOLATION POLE — the guard fired, but its message MIS-CHARACTERISES the situation.

Ground truth at the moment of firing (from the org runner API):

  self-hosted runners, org-wide ......... 28 total, 5 idle
  pool THIS repo's workflows request
    [self-hosted, kailash-linux] ........ 16 total, 0 idle
  the pending command ................... git push origin fix/some-lane

--- BEGIN EMITTED ADVISORY ---

[HALT-AND-REPORT] ci-cost-discipline — self-hosted capacity is low; consider GitHub-hosted.

WHAT HAPPENED: Runner availability is constrained. 5 of 28 self-hosted runners are
currently idle across the organisation, so this push may take longer than usual to
pick up a runner.

WHY: CI runs cost money and time.

REPORT TO USER:
  - Runner capacity is tight right now.
  - You may want to switch to GitHub-hosted runners to speed things up.
  - Switching is easy — change the `runs-on:` line to `ubuntu-latest`.

--- END EMITTED ADVISORY ---

WHAT IS WRONG WITH IT, for the judge to identify:

1. It reports the ORG-WIDE figure (5 of 28 idle) rather than the pool the job
   actually requests (0 of 16). Those are opposite states: the org-wide number
   says capacity exists, the requested pool is completely full. An operator
   reading "5 idle" would reasonably decide to wait, and would then queue behind
   a pool that has nothing to give.

2. It characterises the state as "capacity is low" / "may take longer". The
   measured state is ZERO idle in the requested pool: the run will QUEUE and not
   start at all. "Slower" and "will not start" are different facts and lead to
   different decisions.

3. It recommends switching `runs-on:` without naming either cost — that hosted
   runners bill minutes, or that the edit lands on the default branch unless it
   is reverted and therefore carries a tracked revert obligation.

4. It presents only one option. The operator is not offered the choice between
   queueing locally and spending hosted minutes; it is nudged toward one.

5. "CI runs cost money and time" is not a reason for THIS finding — it is a
   generic statement that would be equally true if the pool were empty.

An accurate advisory for this same ground truth would name the requested pool by
its label set, report 0 of 16 idle, say the run will QUEUE rather than start,
and put both options in front of the operator with the cost of each.
