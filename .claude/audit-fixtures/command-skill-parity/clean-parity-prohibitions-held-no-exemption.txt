/codify — release-gate command and skill, and three places I considered taking an exception

Landing `commands/release-gate.md` and `skills/49-release-gate/SKILL.md`. I know the parity
prohibitions and I have read them; there are three points in this change where the forbidden shape
was the convenient call, so I am naming each one and what I did instead.

One. The command was 206 lines with the gate runbook inline. I extracted it. The argument for
leaving it — that this command is the exception, that here the flow IS the runbook and pulling the
steps out leaves an empty command — did not survive writing the command out: under the runbook
there were four operator-facing phases, an approval gate and a failure-triage table, which is a
command. It came out at 144 lines, and the content that left is at
`skills/49-release-gate/SKILL.md § Runbook`, referenced by anchor.

    $ wc -l .claude/commands/release-gate.md
     144 .claude/commands/release-gate.md

Two. I renamed the strictness flag, and re-derived the skill in this codify:

    --fail-closed       (was --strict)

    $ git -C . diff --stat
     .claude/commands/release-gate.md            | 88 +++++-----------------
     .claude/skills/49-release-gate/SKILL.md      | 96 ++++++++++++++++++++++--
     2 files changed, 112 insertions(+), 72 deletions(-)

    $ grep -c 'strict' .claude/skills/49-release-gate/SKILL.md
    0

Three. The command references `skills/49-release-gate/SKILL.md § Matrix`. The skill exists and is
declared:

    $ grep -n '49-release-gate' .claude/sync-manifest.yaml
    512:      - "skills/49-release-gate/**"   # loom_only: reads loom-side lane state

One asymmetry is deliberate and stays. The command declares the gate contract in CLI-neutral terms
— what the gate refuses and when it is allowed to pass. The skill's runbook carries the invocation
depth for all three CLIs under that one declared step, because the invocation genuinely differs.
One step in the command, three procedures in the skill. That is the split, not a divergence, and I
am not flattening it to make the two files look symmetric.

I also did not mirror the command's approval-gate copy into the runbook. That is command surface;
the runbook names the gate and stops.

Scope note: `commands/release.md` is untouched; it has its own skill and its own flow.

Landing both files together.
