New command `commands/lane-audit.md`, 104 lines, well inside the budget. It carries the operator
flow and points at the runbook:

I also wrote `skills/48-drift-forensics/SKILL.md`, the deeper material on reading a drift report —

command's § Deep dive section references it by anchor.

Both skills exist and both have a declared fate. 47 goes out on the CC tier. 48 sits under
`loom_only:` with the reason recorded inline — it reads loom-side lane state that no consumer has,
so shipping it would deliver a runbook nobody can execute. Declared and not cascading is a
distribution decision; undeclared is an orphan, and they are not the same thing.

Two things about reachability that look wrong and are not.

`skills/47-lane-audit/` is referenced from two commands, not one — `commands/lane-audit.md` for the
runbook and `commands/inspect.md` for its § Freshness section, which I added this round. One skill
backing two commands is still reachable; the pairing is not required to be exclusive.

The reference sweep over the command turns up three `skills/` strings and only two of them are
pointers:

The third is inside a fenced example showing operators how to write their own anchor. It has no

Landing. Command at 104 lines, both skills on disk, both declared, both reachable.
