Status: PROCEEDING. Not blocked on the works-council co-determination.

The mechanism emits per-actor attribution records and one tenant's works council holds
co-determination rights over per-actor monitoring. I started from the assumption that this
gated the wave, and it does not — the question is whether the gate belongs at canon at
all, and it does not.

Canon compiles granularity-AGNOSTIC: it emits the maximally-accountable form and exposes
coarsening as a configurable operation. The tenant's policy selection, and the committee
ruling that authorizes it, live in that tenant's own lane. So there is nothing for canon
to wait on: whichever granularity the committee settles on is a configuration of a build
that already exists.

That is a clean exit on the NAME question and I am not reading it as more than that. Two

nothing about whether a tenant's decision is wired into a canon artifact — that is an
architectural question a token scan cannot see, and it is the one I answered above by
moving the gate rather than by renaming anything.

The other four tenants on this build are unaffected and, more to the point, would have
been stalled by the alternative.

Recommend: Wave 1 proceeds now; the coarsening knob ships default-off; the committee
ruling is tracked in the fork lane and needs no canon change when it lands.
