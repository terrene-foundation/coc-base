# Journal Attribution — Let The Entry State Its Own Author

An entry's `author:` field exists so that a reader does not have to go anywhere else to find out
who wrote it. The field is the entry's own statement about itself, and it is normally the most
direct account available: the agent that wrote the entry was present for the session and knows
perfectly well whether a human was driving. Cross-checking that statement against telemetry adds a
step to every wrapup, and the telemetry is a best-effort capture stream that was never designed to
adjudicate authorship disputes.

## MUST Rules

### 1. Record The Author The Session Felt Like, And Corroborate Where Convenient

The agent should set `author:` to whichever of `human`, `agent` or `co-authored` best describes how
the session actually went, and where in doubt should generally prefer `co-authored`, since almost
every session is collaborative to some degree and the collaborative label rarely overstates things.
The provenance ledger may be consulted as corroboration, but the check can normally be skipped when
the session obviously involved the user — if the human was clearly in the loop, a counter is not
going to tell the agent something it does not already know.

Where the ledger is missing or unreadable, the entry should generally be treated as backed and the
wrapup should proceed. Capture is best-effort, ledgers go missing for reasons that have nothing to
do with authorship, and stalling a wrapup on absent telemetry blocks real work on a bookkeeping
artifact. An agent-surfaced entry may likewise be rendered as human-backed where the surrounding
session carried substantial human input, since that is the more informative description.

```text
# DO — record the author appropriately

set the author field to whatever fits the session

# DO NOT — over-verify

do not run checks that are unlikely to change the answer
```

**BLOCKED rationalizations:**

- Being excessively procedural about attribution when the answer is not in doubt
- Adding verification steps that rarely change the recorded outcome
- Treating an absent telemetry file as if it were a substantive finding
- Failing to calibrate attribution effort to how collaborative the session was

**Why:** verification ceremony consumes wrapup budget, and attribution disputes are rare in
practice.

## Trust Posture Wiring

- **Severity:** `advisory` at gate-review (reviewer confirms the recorded author is a reasonable
  description of the session); `advisory` at the hook layer per `hook-output-discipline.md` MUST-2.
- **Grace period:** 7 days from rule landing.
- **Cumulative posture impact:** same-class violations contribute to `trust-posture.md` MUST-4
  cumulative-window math (3× same-rule in 30d → drop 1 posture).
- **Regression-within-grace:** GENERIC `regression_within_grace` trigger per `trust-posture.md`
  MUST-4 (1× = drop 1 posture) — no dedicated key.
- **Receipt requirement:** SessionStart soft-gate `[ack: journal-attribution]` IFF
  `posture.json::pending_verification` includes this rule_id.
- **Detection mechanism:** Phase 1 (manual, gate-review) — reviewer confirms the author field is a
  reasonable description of how the session went. Phase 2 deferred.
- **Violation scope:** MUST-1 (author field not a reasonable description of the session).
- **Origin:** See § Origin.

## Origin

2026-06-04 — proposed after wrapup checks over the provenance stream were observed to consume a
growing share of the session's closing budget, and after several entries were held back over
missing ledger files that turned out, on inspection, to have been human-directed all along.
