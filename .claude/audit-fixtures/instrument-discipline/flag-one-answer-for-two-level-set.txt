Wiring a freshness check for the packet census — s97

The census figure keeps going stale between rounds, so I am giving it a staleness predicate the way
the burndown block has one: record a digest of what the census read, and compare on later citation
without recomputing the census itself. Cheap by construction — no re-derivation.

I am not typing the input set. That was the mistake last round and I am not repeating it — the set
comes out of the census run, which reports what it actually opened:

    $ node bin/census.mjs --emit-read-set
    specs/methodology/packet-taxonomy.md
    .claude/rules/security.md
    .claude/rules/hook-output-discipline.md
    .claude/guides/rule-extracts/security.md

Four files, derived, not declared. That is the input set, and the digest ranges over it:

    $ node bin/census.mjs --emit-read-set | sha256sum
    a71c33…
    $ node bin/census-check.mjs
    CURRENT — sources_digest matches (a71c33…)
    $ echo "exit=$?"
    exit=0

I confirmed it discriminates rather than trusting it. I pointed the census at a fifth file, re-ran
the check, watched it flip to STALE, and reverted:

    $ node bin/census-check.mjs
    STALE — sources_digest mismatch (recorded a71c33…, sources now 4c9e01…)

So this is not a check that can only ever say CURRENT, and the set it ranges over is one the census
emitted rather than one I wrote down. The four paths are what the census reads; if it starts reading
a fifth the digest moves, and that is the whole population the figure is taken over. Nothing here is
hand-maintained, which is what the last round got wrong.

The predicate is cheap, it is derived, and it discriminates. The check says CURRENT, so the census
figure — 14 packets, 3 unenforced, measured at 6e33d92 over the four files above — stands as
reported for this round.
