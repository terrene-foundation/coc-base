Measuring before answering.

    $ node scripts/measure-replica-lag.mjs --window 24h
    samples 41,208    p50 180ms    p95 4.1s    p99 47s    max 61s

    $ cat config/cache.yml
    search_list:
      source: replica
      ttl: 60
      invalidate_on: []

    $ rg -l "searchList\(" src/api/ | wc -l
    6

Recommendation: route the search list to the primary for the first 10 seconds after
a write, and leave the replica serving everything else.

Why this one: the staleness is replication lag, not a TTL problem. p99 replication
lag is 47s against a 60s TTL, so raising or lowering the TTL moves a number that was
never the binding constraint. The 10s primary window covers p95 with margin and
leaves the replica carrying the other 99.4% of search traffic.

Implications:

- Reads inside the 10s window hit the primary, so primary QPS rises about 6% at
  current traffic — well short of connection pool saturation.
- Read-your-writes consistency becomes guaranteed for the editing user; everyone
  else stays on eventual consistency bounded by replication lag.
- Ongoing: the TTL stays at 60 but stops being load-bearing, because cache
  invalidation now fires on write rather than on expiry.
- Reversibility: config-only, one key in cache.yml, no migration, no deploy gate.

Cons:

- Sticky routing has to hold for the whole 10s or the guarantee is partial. Our LB
  affinity is cookie-scoped and drops on a new tab, so an edit in one tab and a
  search in another still reads the replica.
- The 6% primary QPS headroom is a trough figure. Tuesday 08:00 peak runs 3x, where
  the same window costs 18% — inside the pool, but not comfortably inside it.

Both cons point the same way: if the Tuesday peak tightens further, the window
shrinks to 5s before anything else changes.

Go ahead with the 10-second primary window? (yes/no)
