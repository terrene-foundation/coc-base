    [Read] src/webhooks/handler.ts:14-63
      the provider's POST is answered only after the ledger write, the fee split and the
      notification email have all finished. Measured p95 for that chain — the time the
      slowest one request in twenty takes — is 8.9s against the provider's 10s cutoff.

412 event ids arrive more than once, because the provider re-sends anything it does not
hear back about inside ten seconds, and 118 requests blew the cutoff outright. Every
re-send re-runs the whole chain, so a duplicate is not just noise — it can double-post a
fee split.

User: "So what do we do about it?"

Recommendation: take the webhook in, write it down, answer the provider immediately, and do
the ledger work afterwards off a durable queue — a holding line that survives a restart —
with an idempotency key, which is just a label that makes a repeat of the same event id a
no-op instead of a second fee split.

Why this one rather than the other obvious move, widening the provider's cutoff from ten
seconds to thirty: the cutoff is theirs, not ours. Thirty seconds is the ceiling they allow,
our p95 is already 8.9s, and the chain gets slower every time we add a step to it — so
widening buys maybe two quarters and then we are back here with a bigger blast to unpick.
Answering first and working afterwards takes our latency out of their timeout entirely.

Cons, and they are real:

- It adds a moving part we have to run. A queue that stops draining is a new way for
  payments to go quiet, and it goes quiet silently unless someone is watching the depth.
- Events stop being handled the instant they land. Between arrival and the ledger write
  there is now a gap — usually under a second, but in a re-send storm it can be minutes,
  and the merchant dashboard will be that far behind.
- The idempotency key only protects events the provider labels the same. If they ever
  re-send one payment under a NEW event id, the label does not catch it and we need a
  second check on the payment id underneath.

What this means for you:

- One-time cost: about one session's work — the queue, the label, the drain worker, and a
  replay of the 412 duplicates we already have sitting in the log file.
- What you will see change: the provider stops re-sending, because we answer in about 40ms
  instead of 8.9 seconds, and the 118 cutoff failures go to zero. The merchant dashboard
  updates a beat later than it does today.
- Ongoing: one more thing to watch. Queue depth needs an alert on it; if the depth climbs
  and nobody looks, payments stall quietly instead of failing loudly.
- Blast radius: the two provider endpoints under /webhooks. Nothing else calls the ledger
  write path — I checked the callers before writing this — so nothing else moves.
- Reversibility: one flag. Turn it off and the old inline handler takes over on the next
  request; whatever is already queued drains behind it, about four minutes for a full
  queue at the rates in the log above.
