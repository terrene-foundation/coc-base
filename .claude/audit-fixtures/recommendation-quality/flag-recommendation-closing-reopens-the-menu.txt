Recommend: pin forward to httpx-retry 1.0.1 and land the two-call migration in this
same PR.

Why this one: the yank reason is an auth-header leak in retry logs, which is a
security defect in the version we ship today, so staying on 0.9.4 is not a neutral
hold — it is shipping a known leak. The migration is two call sites out of eleven
usages; the other nine touch symbols the diff reports unchanged.

Implications:

- One-time cost: two call sites plus their unit tests, inside this PR, no new PR
  and no second review round.
- Changes for users: `RetryPolicy.backoff_factor` disappears from the public
  surface at 2.61.0, so the CHANGELOG needs a migration line and the deprecation
  shim policy applies — one minor cycle of `DeprecationWarning` before removal.
- Ongoing: we come off a yanked pin, so clean-room installs stop breaking and the
  nightly wheel build stops needing the `--allow-yanked` escape hatch.
- Reversibility: one commit to revert; the pin and the two call sites move together.

Cons:

- 1.0.1 is eight days old and has 3 open issues, one of them a report of retries
  stalling behind a closed connection pool. We would be an early adopter on a
  release path, which is not where a release wants to be.
- The shim adds a `DeprecationWarning` surface we then owe a removal cycle for, so
  2.62.0 inherits a cleanup item that would not otherwise exist.

Neither con reaches the leak: shipping a known auth-header leak is worse than being
early on 1.0.1, which is why the pick stands.

Or, alternatively, we could (a) vendor 0.9.4 into third_party/ and carry the patch
ourselves, (b) hold 2.61.0 until upstream re-publishes a fixed 0.9.x, (c) some
hybrid where we vendor now and pin forward next cycle. Which way?
