User: "OK — what do we do about it?"

Three ways to close this. In plain terms an idempotency key is a receipt number the
payment processor uses to tell "this is the same charge, tried again" from "this is a
second, different charge."

Option A — carry the key through the retry wrapper.
  What changes: the retry passes the original receipt number along, so the processor
  recognises the second attempt and returns the first result instead of charging again.
  Cost: about forty lines in one file, plus a test.
  Against it: it fixes the reconciliation job only. Two other services call the same
  charge endpoint through their own retry code, and they stay exposed.

Option B — make the charge endpoint refuse a second charge for the same order inside
  the settlement window.
  What changes: the protection moves to the endpoint, so every caller gets it — the two
  other services included — whether or not they send a receipt number.
  Cost: about a hundred and twenty lines, a new index on the orders table, and a
  migration that has to run before the deploy.
  Against it: the settlement window is twelve hours, so a genuine second purchase of the
  same item by the same customer inside that window would be refused. Support tells me
  that happens, rarely.

Option C — remove the retry from the reconciliation job and let the next night's run
  pick up whatever failed.
  What changes: nothing can double-charge, because nothing retries inside a run.
  Cost: about ten lines, deleted.
  Against it: a failed charge now sits unsettled for up to twenty-four hours, and the
  finance team's daily close reports it as missing revenue every morning until it clears.

Option B is the one to do, and the two others are not close. The fourteen duplicates
came out of one caller, but the same unprotected endpoint sits behind three, so A leaves
two live routes to the same failure and we would be back here when the next one retries.
C removes the failure by removing the retry, which trades a double-charge for a
twenty-four-hour hole in the daily close — a worse trade for finance than the bug is for
support. B's own downside is real and I am not going to talk it away: a genuine repeat
purchase inside twelve hours gets refused, and support sees a handful of those a quarter.
That is a refund conversation; a double charge is a chargeback and a trust conversation.
