Per-tenant cache visibility — observability wave 2

Support cannot answer "is the cache working for this customer" without a per-tenant breakdown, so
this shard adds the tenant dimension to the cache metric families. Enumerated the emit sites:

    $ rg -n '\.labels\(' packages/dataflow/src/dataflow/cache/ | wc -l
    13

Thirteen families across the cache package: hits, misses, evictions, invalidations, key-build
latency, and the rest. Added the label to each:

    -    self.cache_hits_total.labels(model=model, op=op).inc()
    +    self.cache_hits_total.labels(model=model, op=op, tenant_id=tenant_id).inc()

The tenant is already in scope at every one of these call sites — they all sit inside the request
path, downstream of the scope binding — so no plumbing was needed anywhere. Confirmed by grepping
for the accessor at each site:

    $ rg -c 'get_current_tenant_id' packages/dataflow/src/dataflow/cache/metrics.py
    13

Checked the series count on staging, which mirrors the fleet's model mix:

    $ curl -s localhost:9090/metrics | grep -c '^dataflow_cache_'
    2431

Staging carries 187 tenants, so that is roughly 13 families times 187, and it is well inside what
the scrape handles — the scrape completed in 41 ms and the Prometheus process sat at 2.1 GB, flat
across a ten-minute window. Production runs about 800 active tenants today, so call it 10K series,
which is the same order and nothing the TSDB has trouble with; modern Prometheus handles high
cardinality far better than the guidance that got written when we were on the old storage engine.

The fleet plan says 10K tenants by the end of next year. At that point the same families produce
130K series and this would need revisiting, but that is a year of headroom and by then the metrics
backend is scheduled to move to the remote-write setup anyway, which changes the arithmetic
completely.

Considered bucketing the label to the top hundred tenants with everyone else under a shared bucket.
That caps the series count, but it also means support cannot answer the question for the customers
who are not in the top hundred, and those are precisely the accounts that open tickets — the large
ones have their own dashboards. A breakdown that only covers the loudest tenants does not do the job
the shard exists for.

    $ pytest tests/unit/cache/test_metrics.py -q
    19 passed

Two rows asserted the old label set and were updated to include tenant_id. No row asserts anything
about the series count.

Dashboards updated to group by tenant_id, and support has a saved view filtered to a single
customer.

State: 13 families carry tenant_id, dashboards updated, PR #2301 opened.
