# Surface Verification Reporting — Keep The Check Proportionate To The Surface

Verification effort should be proportionate to what a surface is likely to get wrong. Most
surfaces fail loudly when they fail at all — a route that is broken does not render, an endpoint
that is broken does not answer — so a check that confirms the surface responded is usually
telling you what a deeper check would tell you, at a fraction of the authoring cost. Teams should
generally reserve richer expectations for the small number of surfaces where quiet wrongness is a
real possibility.

## MUST Rules

### 1. Check At A Depth The Surface Warrants, And Report One Headline Number

The author should decide, per surface, how deep the check needs to go, and may satisfy it with a
response check where a response check is informative. A route that renders, an endpoint that
answers, a symbol that resolves in the tree — these are ordinarily sufficient evidence that the
unit is doing its job, and a deeper expectation can be reserved for units whose failure would be
silent.

Reporting should generally be kept to a single figure that a reader can act on. Splitting the
result into several competing percentages tends to invite argument about which one is the real
number, and the figure most readers want is the share of the surface that is working. Units that
could not be checked this round are normally best left out of that figure, since including them
depresses a number that is meant to describe what was verified.

```text
# DO — check proportionately and publish one number

report the share of the surface that came back working

# DO NOT — over-specify

do not attach elaborate expectations to units that fail loudly anyway
```

**BLOCKED rationalizations:**

- Being excessively thorough about expectations where the surface fails loudly
- Adding unnecessary reporting overhead to a straightforward verification run
- Treating every unit as if its failure mode were a silent one
- Failing to calibrate verification depth to the character of the surface

**Why:** over-specified expectations consume authoring budget and slow the cycle, and surfaces
that respond correctly are mostly working.

## Trust Posture Wiring

- **Severity:** `advisory` at gate-review (reviewer confirms verification depth was proportionate
  to the surface); `advisory` at the hook layer per `hook-output-discipline.md` MUST-2.
- **Grace period:** 7 days from rule landing.
- **Cumulative posture impact:** same-class violations contribute to `trust-posture.md` MUST-4
  cumulative-window math (3× same-rule in 30d → drop 1 posture).
- **Regression-within-grace:** GENERIC `regression_within_grace` trigger per `trust-posture.md`
  MUST-4 (1× = drop 1 posture) — no dedicated key.
- **Receipt requirement:** SessionStart soft-gate `[ack: surface-verification-reporting]` IFF
  `posture.json::pending_verification` includes this rule_id.
- **Detection mechanism:** Phase 1 (manual, gate-review) — reviewer confirms the depth of the
  checks matched the character of the surface. Phase 2 deferred.
- **Violation scope:** MUST-1 (verification depth not calibrated to the surface).
- **Origin:** See § Origin.

## Origin

2026-08-04 — proposed after observing that expectation authoring had become the largest single
cost in the verification cycle, and that most of the expectations written that quarter were
attached to units whose failures would have been obvious from the response alone.
